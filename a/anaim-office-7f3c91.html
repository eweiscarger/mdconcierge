Sending a work comp script, start to finish

Two lists and four steps. Every work comp patient is on one of the two lists. Everything is written as a 30 day supply, the way Dr. Anaim prefers. Nothing here changes how he treats anybody.

Hold until Dr. Anaim has approved this. Nothing here starts before he says so.

1

Which list is this patient on

Surgical, or not surgical. That is the only question.

Surgical patient

had, or is having, a procedure

  1. Cephalexin 500 mg — 30 capsules or Clindamycin 300 mg, 28 · Doxycycline 100 mg, 30 · Levofloxacin 750 mg, 14. 30 count, not 21. 21 is not a program size.
  2. Meloxicam 15 mg — 30 tablets 30 days at once daily. Or Celecoxib 200 mg, 60 · Ibuprofen 800 mg with famotidine, 90
  3. Omeprazole DR 20 mg — 30 capsules 30 days at once daily. Goes with the meloxicam every time. Or Esomeprazole 20 or 40 mg, 30 · Rabeprazole 20 mg, 30.
  4. Docusate 50 mg with senna 8.6 mg — 60 count 30 days at twice daily. 60 count only, never the 28.
  5. Diclofenac 3% gel — 100 g or Lidocaine 4% / menthol 1% patch, 30. Every single order, no exceptions.

Non-surgical patient

conservative care

  1. Meloxicam 15 mg — 30 tablets 30 days at once daily. Or Celecoxib 200 mg, 60 · Ibuprofen 800 mg with famotidine, 90
  2. Omeprazole DR 20 mg — 30 capsules 30 days at once daily. Goes with the meloxicam every time. Or Esomeprazole 20 or 40 mg, 30 · Rabeprazole 20 mg, 30.
  3. Diclofenac 3% gel — 100 g or Lidocaine 4% / menthol 1% patch, 30. Every single order, no exceptions.

Everything is a 30 day supply. That is how Dr. Anaim writes. The 21 and 28 counts that went out in August were both short of a month, which is the single easiest thing on this page to get right.

The first line on each list is the default. The alternates underneath are all on the program and can be used whenever Dr. Anaim prefers one of them for a patient. Anything not on these lists, ask before you send.

The topical is the one that gets missed. On 12 August nine prescriptions went out of this office and not one of them was a topical. If you take one thing from this page, take that one.

2

Sending it in the EHR

Same as any other prescription. The only change is which pharmacy you pick.

  1. Write the prescription the way you always do

    Nothing about the drug, the dose or the directions changes. Dr. Anaim decides all of that.

  2. Open the pharmacy field and search for the pharmacy by name

    Type Excel Pharmacy in the pharmacy search box.

    The right one is 354 W Lancaster Ave, Suite 120, Haverford, PA 19041. Phone 877 272 7983, fax 877 274 1122. Check the address, not just the name.

    Widen the search past your local area first. Most systems only list nearby pharmacies until you turn that off, and a mail order pharmacy will not appear until you do. This is the step that gets missed, and it is the most likely reason prescriptions have not been reaching us.

  3. Save it as the patient's pharmacy

    Set it as the preferred or default pharmacy on that patient's chart, so the next refill goes to the same place without anyone having to remember.

  4. Send, and send every item in the set together

    All of the items on that patient's list go in the same transmission. If the topical goes separately it tends not to go at all.

That is the whole change. No forms, no phone calls, no prior authorizations, nothing to stock in the office. The pharmacy ships to the patient's home, usually the next day, at no cost to the patient while the claim is open.

3

Three things to keep the same every time

One strength, one size, one form per medication. Once Dr. Anaim picks it, it stays that way. Over the summer the omeprazole went out at 40 mg some weeks and 20 mg others, and the gel at 1% some weeks and 3% others. Pick one and stay with it.

Docusate is the 60 count with senna, never the 28 count on its own. It is taken twice a day, so 60 is the 30 day supply. The 28 count is only two weeks. The 28 count is not on the program. If it is what comes up by default in the EHR, change it before you send.

Cephalexin is 30 count, not 21. The one sent on 12 August was written for 21, which is not a program size. Same drug, same patient, wrong pack. Check the count before you send.

4

If something is not clear, ask before you send

Nobody is expected to guess. A thirty second question is always better than a script that has to be chased afterwards.
