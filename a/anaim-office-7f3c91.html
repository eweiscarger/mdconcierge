Sending a work comp script, start to finish

Two lists and four steps. Every work comp patient is on one of the two lists. Everything is a 30 day supply, the way Dr. Anaim writes. Nothing here changes how he treats anybody.

Hold until Dr. Anaim has approved this. Nothing here starts before he says so.

1

The topical is the one that gets missed. On 12 August nine prescriptions went out of this office and not one was a topical.

2

Sending it in the EHR

Same as any other prescription. The only change is which pharmacy you pick.

  1. Write the prescription the way you always do

    Nothing about the drug, the dose or the directions changes. Dr. Anaim decides all of that.

  2. Search the pharmacy directory for Excel Pharmacy

    The right one is 354 W Lancaster Ave, Suite 120, Haverford, PA 19041. Phone 877 272 7983, fax 877 274 1122. Check the address, not just the name.

    Widen the search past your local area first. Most systems only list nearby pharmacies until you turn that off, and a mail order pharmacy will not appear until you do. This is the step that gets missed, and it is the most likely reason prescriptions have not been reaching us.

  3. Save it as the patient's pharmacy

    Set it as the preferred pharmacy on that patient's chart, so the next refill goes to the same place without anyone having to remember.

  4. Send every item in the set together

    All of the items on that patient's list go in the same transmission. If the topical goes separately it tends not to go at all.

That is the whole change. No forms, no phone calls, nothing stocked in the office. The pharmacy ships to the patient's home, usually the next day, at no cost to the patient while the claim is open.

3

Three things to keep the same every time

One strength, one size, one form per medication. Once Dr. Anaim picks it, it stays that way. Over the summer the omeprazole went out at 40 mg some weeks and 20 mg others, and the gel at 1% some weeks and 3% others.

Docusate is the 60 count with senna. It is taken twice a day, so 60 is the 30 day supply. The 28 count is only two weeks and is not a program size.

Cephalexin is 30 count, not 21. The one sent on 12 August was written for 21, which is not a program size. Same drug, same patient, wrong pack. Check the count before you send.

Keep this on your phone

It lives at a web address rather than in a file, so it is always the current version. Two taps and it sits on your home screen like an app.

iPhone. Tap the share button at the bottom of Safari, the square with the arrow coming out of it. Scroll down the list and tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Android. Tap the three dots at the top right of Chrome, then Add to Home screen, then Install or Add.

Either one. If you would rather not, just bookmark it, or leave the text where it is and open it from there.
