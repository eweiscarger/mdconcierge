Where a work comp script goes, and what to put on it

The pharmacy, the four steps for sending it, and the six things that hold a prescription up if they are missing. Nothing here changes what Dr. Grossinger prescribes. He decides that, every time, for every patient.

1

The pharmacy

This is the whole change. Everything else about a work comp prescription stays as it is.

Widen the pharmacy search past your local area first. This is the step offices miss. Most systems only list pharmacies near you until you turn that off, and a mail order pharmacy will not appear until you do.

2

Sending it

  1. Search the pharmacy directory for Excel Pharmacy

    Check the Haverford address, not just the name.

  2. Save it as the patient's preferred pharmacy

    Then refills go to the same place without anyone having to remember.

  3. E-prescribe it, exactly as you would to any other pharmacy

    Or fax to 877 274 1122 with a completed demographic sheet.

  4. Include the six items below

    A prescription missing any of them is the one that sits.

3

The six things that hold a script up

On the demographic sheet, or in the notes if you are e-prescribing.

  • Diagnosis code
  • Claim number
  • Allergies, or NKA
  • Date of injury
  • Work comp carrier
  • Adjuster, with a phone number
4

What happens after you send it

Excel's advocate team calls the patient within 24 hours to confirm insurance, the prescription and the delivery address. The call comes from area code 484 or 267, which is worth telling the patient, otherwise they let it ring.

From there it ships to their home, usually next day. No copay and nothing out of pocket on covered work comp medications, no trip to a counter, and no prior authorization landing on you.

Nothing is stocked here, handled here, or billed from here. MDRx does the billing and the collections.
