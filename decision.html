The decision, in plain English

What the Pennsylvania Supreme Court settled, and what it means for you

On June 16, 2026, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court decided 700 Pharmacy v. Bureau of Workers' Compensation Fee Review Hearing Office. Attorney Daniel Siegel argued and won it. Here is the whole thing without the legalese.

What the case was about

Some insurers had refused to pay for a work-comp patient's prescriptions when the prescribing physician had a financial interest in the pharmacy, arguing that Pennsylvania's Workers' Compensation Act anti-referral provision made it off limits. 700 Pharmacy challenged that.

What the Court held

  • The anti-referral ban applies only to eight specifically listed services: laboratory, physical therapy, rehabilitation, chiropractic, radiation oncology, psychometric, home infusion, and diagnostic imaging.
  • Prescription drugs and pharmacy services are not on that list, and the phrase "goods or services" is not a catch-all that sweeps them in.
  • So self-referrals for work-comp prescription drugs are not prohibited under the current law, and insurers cannot deny payment on self-referral grounds.

What it means for physicians

A physician, individually or as a practice, can take part in the economics of the work-comp prescriptions they already write, supporting continuity of care, without violating the anti-referral provision. Nothing about how you prescribe changes.

The honest limits

The eight designated services above stay restricted. This applies to workers-comp (and auto) claims only, never Medicare, Medicaid, or any federal claim. The Court also noted that if the legislature ever wants to add prescription drugs to the ban, it would have to do so explicitly. For now, the law stands as the Court interpreted it.
