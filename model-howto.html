How to use your dispensing analysis

About a minute. Every number in it is yours to change.

Opening it

  1. Click the link in your email. It asks who you are: press Continue as your own name and you are straight in. Your device remembers you, so it only ever asks once.
  2. Sharing it with a partner or your office manager? Just forward the email. They press I'm someone else, give a name and email, and they are in too. Nobody is locked out and there is no password to pass around.
The analysis is confidential and prepared for one practice, so each person is asked to say who they are. Anyone who skips that step still gets the full calculator, but the product cost column stays hidden until they do.

Using it

  1. Pick your state. Every state sets its own workers' compensation pharmacy fee schedule and they are nowhere near each other. Pennsylvania pays AWP plus 10%. New York pays AWP minus 12% on brand and minus 20% on generic. Choosing your state sets the multiplier for you, and you can type your own number over it whenever you like. Show all states lists every one.
  2. Set your monthly patient volume — how many workers' compensation patients you treat in a typical month.
  3. Build your mix from the formulary. Scroll to the full formulary at the bottom, then filter by category or route, search by name, or click any column heading to sort. Sorting by reimbursement is the quick way to see what is worth dispensing. Tick the products you actually dispense and press "Add to my mix."
  4. Set how many of each an average patient receives per month, and read the estimate. It updates as you type.

What the numbers assume

The defaults are deliberately conservative and every one of them is editable:

  • 80% collections on oral scripts, 70% on topicals.
  • Your practice keeps 65% of net.
  • The practice carries the cost of the medication plus a per-script dispensing fee. Both come out before the split, and both are already deducted from the figure you see.

The formulary lists the most common medications only. Thousands more are available, so if you dispense something that is not on the list, say so and it gets added.
