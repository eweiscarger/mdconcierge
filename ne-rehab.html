The law

Why this is possible now

“The plain language of the statute makes clear the General Assembly did not intend for ‘goods or services’ to be a catchall category bringing non-enumerated medical services within the Anti-Referral Provision's prohibition on self-referrals.” 700 Pharmacy v. Bureau of Workers' Compensation Fee Review Hearing Office · Pa. Supreme Court · June 16, 2026 · 5–2

Daniel Siegel, who argued the case, put the effect in three lines: physicians may refer to a pharmacy they hold an interest in; physician-owned pharmacies may seek payment on work comp claims; insurers may not deny payment on self-referral grounds. Alice Gosfield's June note maps the limits — participation runs through the claim, not dispensing, because Pennsylvania's 2014 bar on physicians dispensing to work comp patients is untouched.

The dispensing tool

Build the practice's own formulary and watch the estimate move. It opens under your name, so it is yours rather than a generic list.

  1. Set the assumptions at the top. Patients per month and your state, which pulls the multiplier straight from that state's work comp pharmacy fee schedule. Dispensing fee, shipping, collection percentages and the split are all editable if you want to be more conservative than the defaults.
  2. Build your mix. Add the medications this practice actually writes and the estimate updates as you go.
  3. Account for refills. All three are 30-day supplies, so a patient on an open claim fills once a month for as long as the claim runs. The pharmacy calls before every refill and ships nothing if the patient declines — these are consented fills, not an auto-ship.
  4. The full formulary is at the bottom, searchable by product, category or route, with wholesale cost shown and the injection kits below it.
