MDRx workers’ compensation pharmacy

Sending a work comp prescription

For whoever handles the scripts. Two minutes to set up, then it is one click a visit. Nothing about how you prescribe changes.

The rule

When the visit is work comp, the prescription goes to the work comp pharmacy rather than the patient’s retail pharmacy. That is the whole change.

Your email, to open this

This names the pharmacy and carries the formulary, so it is not public. Put in your email and it opens.

Two ways to send it

  1. E-prescribe. Route the script to the pharmacy from your existing e-prescribing software, exactly as you would to any other pharmacy.
  2. Or fax it, on the fax number above, together with a completed demographic sheet.

What the demographic sheet must include

This is what holds prescriptions up. Six items:

  • Diagnosis code
  • Claim number
  • Allergies, or NKA
  • Date of injury
  • Work comp insurance carrier
  • Adjuster and phone

What happens next

The pharmacy’s advocate team calls the patient within 24 hours to confirm insurance, the prescription and the delivery address. The call comes from area code 484 or 267, which is worth telling the patient so they answer it.

From there it ships to their home. No copay and nothing paid out of pocket for covered work comp medications, no trip to a counter, and no prior authorization landing on your staff. Patients get their medication while the claim is open.

What this office never does

Nothing is stocked here, nothing is handled here, and nothing is billed from here. MDRx does the billing and the collections.

The formulary

The full work comp formulary, oral and topical. Other work comp medications are available on request, so if what you write is not listed, ask rather than assume.
